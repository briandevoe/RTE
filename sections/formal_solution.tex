\section{Formal Solution of the Radiative Transfer Equation}

Consider the radiative transfer equation along a fixed direction in a plane-parallel medium. 
Along a ray parameterized by path length $s$, the equation may be written as

\begin{equation}
\frac{dI(s)}{ds} = -\kappa(s) I(s) + j(s).
\end{equation}

This is a first-order linear ordinary differential equation. 
We rewrite it in standard linear form:

\begin{equation}
\frac{dI}{ds} + \kappa(s) I = j(s).
\end{equation}

The formal solution of the radiative transfer equation follows the classical development of Chandrasekhar \cite{chandrasekhar1960}.

The integrating factor is

\begin{equation}
\mu(s) = \exp\left( \int_0^s \kappa(s') \, ds' \right).
\end{equation}

Multiplying through:

\begin{equation}
\mu \frac{dI}{ds} + \mu \kappa I = \mu j.
\end{equation}

The left-hand side becomes a total derivative:

\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{ds} \left[ \mu(s) I(s) \right] = \mu(s) j(s).
\end{equation}

Integrating from $0$ to $s$:

\begin{equation}
\mu(s) I(s) - I(0) = \int_0^s \mu(s') j(s') \, ds'.
\end{equation}

Solving for $I(s)$:

\begin{equation}
I(s) = e^{-\int_0^s \kappa(u)\,du}
\left[
I(0) + \int_0^s e^{\int_0^{s'} \kappa(u)\,du} j(s')\, ds'
\right].
\end{equation}
