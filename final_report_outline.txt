FIG. 01 — Leaf Angle Distributions (Slide 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: The six leaf inclination angle distribution functions
                 g_L(theta_L) plotted vs. theta_L from 0 to 90 degrees.
                 Distributions: Uniform, Planophile, Erectophile,
                 Plagiophile, Extremophile, Spherical.
  Physics: Each distribution describes the statistical orientation of leaves
           in the canopy. Planophile canopies have mostly horizontal leaves;
           erectophile canopies have mostly vertical leaves.
  Code: code/figure01.ipynb  ← DONE
  Status: COMPLETE

FIG. 02 — G-Function for Various Leaf Angle Distributions (Slide 4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: The G-function G(theta) vs. solar/view zenith angle (0-90°)
                 for the five main LADs (Uniform, Planophile, Erectophile,
                 Plagiophile, Extremophile). Horizontal reference at G=0.5.
  Physics: G(theta) is the Ross-Nilson geometry factor — the mean projection
           of unit leaf area onto a plane normal to the direction theta.
           All distributions satisfy: (1/2pi) * integral G dOmega = 0.5.
  Code: code/figure02.ipynb  ← DONE
  Status: COMPLETE

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIG. 03 — Scattering Phase Function for Uniform LAD (Slide 5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: The bi-Lambertian area scattering phase function
                 Gamma_LD,U(cos_beta) for uniform leaf angle distribution,
                 plotted for several transmittance-to-albedo ratios (tau/omega
                 from 0.0 to 0.5).
  Physics: The phase function governs how a leaf scatters incident radiation.
           Forward scattering (transmission) vs. backward scattering
           (reflection) balance depends on leaf optical properties.

  Status: COMPLETE

FIG. 04 — Uncollided Radiation Profile, RED Band (Slide 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: Downward and upward uncollided irradiance vs. cumulative LAI
                 (depth into canopy) for the RED wavelength band.
  Physics: Uncollided radiation follows Beer-Lambert attenuation — photons
           that have not yet scattered off any leaf surface. Downward direct
           beam decays exponentially; upward component comes from ground
           reflection.
  Code: code/dom_uncollided.py  → outputs code/uncollided_dom_RED.png
  Status: PNG EXISTS — verify image is final and use in report.

FIG. 05 — Uncollided Radiation Profile, NIR Band (Slide 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: Same as Fig. 04 but for the NIR wavelength band.
  Physics: NIR has higher leaf albedo (rho_L + tau_L ~ 0.9) compared to RED
           (~0.09 for red-absorbing vegetation), so the uncollided profile
           decays at a different rate.

FIG. 08 — BHR as Function of LAI and Soil Brightness (Slide 9)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  What it shows: Bihemispherical Reflectance (BHR) vs. LAI for two soil
                 conditions: dark soil (rho_g = 0.01) and bright soil
                 (rho_g = 0.3). At least RED and NIR bands shown.
  Physics: As LAI increases, canopy "hides" the soil. For dark soil, BHR
           increases with LAI (leaves are brighter than soil). For bright
           soil, BHR may decrease or plateau as leaves replace bright soil.
           Asymptotes to canopy-only reflectance at very high LAI.
